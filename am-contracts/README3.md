# The shape of Armenia's procurement year, 2018 to 2026: January, not December

Reestri data story #3, built on the same `am-contracts` index as stories #1 and #2 (Apify KV store
`tb2jS3TGvqYDEkf5Q`). This run used a fresher snapshot, generated **2026-09-06T04:03:42.289Z**: 235,570
unique contracts indexed, against 238,624 reported by PPCM. Stories #1 and #2 used the 2026-08-29 snapshot
(236,159 contracts), so headline totals here differ slightly from theirs by design; the method is the same.

Everything is aggregate. Buyers are state and community bodies. Supplier names and tax IDs appear only for
legal entities; individual entrepreneurs are counted in totals but never listed.

## The premise was wrong, and that is the finding

We set out to test the familiar end-of-year procurement rush: budget money that must be spent before
31 December, signed in a hurry, more of it single source, more of it under "urgent" procedure forms.
Armenia's registry does not show that.

Over the eight complete years 2018 to 2025 (192,369 contracts, 3,958,215,584,070 AMD):

1. **December is the busiest month by contract count and an ordinary month by money.** December holds
   20,094 contracts, 10.45 percent of the count, against 8.33 percent for an even month. But its
   424,043,419,447 AMD is 10.71 percent of the value, an index of 1.29. **January holds
   638,976,946,239 AMD, 16.14 percent of the value, an index of 1.94.** January carries 1.51 times
   December's money. January and February together hold 29.02 percent of the value on 19.72 percent
   of the contracts.
2. **December's value share is below an even month in four of the eight complete years**: 3.46 percent
   in 2018, 6.74 in 2022, 7.42 in 2023, 6.10 in 2025. It clears an even month only in 2019 (9.60),
   2020 (29.03), 2021 (19.58) and 2024 (9.90). The pooled December bump rests on two years.
3. **No buyer of any size runs a December cliff.** Of 129 buyers that signed at least 50 contracts worth
   at least 500,000,000 AMD in 2018 to 2025, the median share of value signed in December is
   **7.69 percent, below an even month**. One buyer is above 50 percent, three above a third, eight above
   a quarter. The most concentrated is the Migration Service of the Ministry of Territorial Administration
   and Infrastructure at 83.07 percent, on a total of 1,548,737,498 AMD across 386 contracts. Next is the
   Administration of the Council of Public Broadcaster at 39.37 percent of 55,522,992,647 AMD, and the
   State Protocol Service of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs at 35.03 percent of 1,591,606,902 AMD.
4. **December contracts are not bigger.** Median 450,000 AMD in December against 434,850 for the rest of
   the year; mean 21,150,353 against 20,609,701. 245 distinct buyers sign in December, 313 across the
   other eleven months.
5. **December is not where urgent procedures live.** 29.79 percent of December value uses an urgent
   procedure form, against 29.43 percent for the rest of the year, and 4.03 percent of December contracts
   against 3.82 percent. Pooled by month, December ranks ninth of twelve on the urgent share of its own value; eight
   months run higher, from February at 43.68 percent down to July at 32.54.
6. **December is more single source, and that comes mostly from two years.** 36.77 percent of December
   contracts are single source against 32.65 for the rest; by value 35.41 against 20.20. But December 2020
   ran 71.48 percent single source by value against 36.46 for the rest of 2020, and December 2021 46.79
   against 14.83. In 2023 and 2024 December was *less* single source by value than the rest of the year
   (11.41 against 22.10, and 5.21 against 7.00).
7. **Within December there is a real last-week rush, and it is small next to the year.** 5,875 contracts,
   29.24 percent of December's count, are signed on 22 December or later; they carry 231,700,965,982 AMD,
   54.64 percent of December value but **5.85 percent of the whole year's**.

## What the January peak actually is

January is not a rush. It is the state medical order. In January 2018 the Ministry of Health signed
73.19 percent of the month's value and 89.24 percent of the month's value was awarded single source;
January 2019, 71.92 percent and 87.82; January 2020, 68.18 and 83.55; January 2022, 56.10 and 60.92;
January 2023, 56.15 and 59.20. Pooled over 2018 to 2025, 61.25 percent of January value is single source,
the highest of any month, and only 3.37 percent is urgent, the lowest of any month. Story #1 flagged this
same mechanism from the other direction: PPCM records state-order payments to public hospitals as
single-source contracts, and they are signed at the top of the budget year.

Since 2024 the peak has moved from January to February and changed character. February 2024 was
14.69 percent of that year's value with 51.04 percent of the month urgent and the City Planning Committee
taking 46.35 percent of it. February 2025 was 27.79 percent of the year's value, 70.72 percent of the
month urgent, with the Armenian Territorial Development Fund taking 66.08 percent. February 2026 is
45.94 percent of a year that runs only to 6 September, again with the Fund at 64.39 percent, so that
figure will fall as the year fills in and is not used in any finding.

## The urgent procedure is the story December is not

"Urgent" is two PPCM procedure forms: ՀՄԱ urgent single source and ՀԲՄ urgent open tender. Across 2018 to
2026 they cover **8,107 contracts, 3.79 percent of the count, worth 1,247,263,397,100 AMD, 27.59 percent
of the value**. The share of value has climbed: 10.70 percent in 2018, 26.29 in 2019, 17.41 in 2020,
31.78 in 2021, 20.20 in 2022, 24.94 in 2023, **42.07 in 2024**, 38.79 in 2025, 14.39 in 2026 to date.

The two forms moved in opposite directions. Urgent open tender went from 147 contracts worth
22,291,130,513 AMD in 2018 to 723 worth 333,983,305,637 AMD in 2024 and 919 worth 267,079,858,915 AMD in
2025. Urgent single source peaked at 1,069 contracts in 2023 and 38,988,242,748 AMD in 2021, and was
13,432,726,568 AMD in 2024. The growth is in accelerated tenders for large construction, not in
accelerated sole-supplier awards.

**Two buyers hold 61.40 percent of all urgent value.** The Ministry of Territorial Administration and
Infrastructure has 925 urgent contracts worth 418,145,449,528 AMD, 58.02 percent of everything it spent
through PPCM. The Armenian Territorial Development Fund has 764 worth 347,643,256,065 AMD, 64.75 percent
of its own spend, of which 345,437,487,236 AMD is urgent open tender. Add the City Planning Committee
(255 contracts, 154,228,319,463 AMD, 33.40 percent of its own spend) and the top three hold 73.76 percent.
Ashtarak community municipality runs the highest share of its own money through urgent procedures of any
buyer in the top 30: 72.67 percent, 18,301,033,681 AMD over 30 contracts, all of it urgent open tender.

## Method

- Unit of analysis: one PPCM contract record (`contractId`), deduplicated across shards. Year and month
  come from `dateSigned`. Value is `contractValueTotal` in AMD, PPCM's stated total; `latestValue` is the
  amount after amendments and is used only to identify keying errors.
- A buyer is `buyerTaxId`, falling back to a hash of the name. Buyer names are printed as PPCM stores them,
  so the same body can appear under Armenian and English spellings across years; rows are keyed by tax ID,
  so totals hold.
- Single source is PPCM's own procedure forms ՄԱ and ՀՄԱ, exactly as in story #1.
- Urgent is PPCM's own procedure forms ՀՄԱ (urgent single source) and ՀԲՄ (urgent open tender). These are
  the only two labels in the registry whose English name begins with "urgent". `summary3.json` carries the
  full procedure vocabulary for this window with the classification each label received, and an
  `unmappedUrgentLabels` field that is empty on this run; if PPCM adds an urgent form, that field fills.
- Note the overlap: ՀՄԱ is counted both as single source and as urgent, because it is both. The two
  families are never added together.
- An "even month" is 100 divided by 12, 8.33 percent. The index columns divide a month's share by that.
- Table 11 and Table 12 use the eight complete years 2018 to 2025 only. The snapshot stops on
  6 September 2026, so 2026 has no December and cannot enter a December comparison. Table 10 and Table 13
  show 2026 as year to date and label it.
- Table 12 floor: at least 50 contracts and at least 500,000,000 AMD of value over 2018 to 2025. 313
  buyers were considered; 129 clear the floor. Without a floor, a body with three contracts reaches 100
  percent in December and the table says nothing.
- The 22 December cut is a calendar cut, not a working-day cut. Armenian public bodies work up to and
  including 31 December, so the block is the last ten calendar days of the year.

## Exclusions, stated in full

- **Value outliers.** Six records carry keyed-in totals from 138,515,676,294 to 1,660,030,208,638,300 AMD
  while their `latestValue` sits below 1.5 billion. Records above 50,000,000,000 AMD are excluded from
  every value sum and kept in every count. All six are listed with their dates and procedures in
  `summary3.json` under `cappedRows`. They are: 2026-08-03 electronic auction; 2023-09-05 urgent single
  source; two 2023-10-12 urgent open tenders; 2025-06-17 request for quotation; 2022-09-01 urgent open
  tender. Below the cap, some records still carry a total far above their latest value; they are left as
  published and inflate value figures slightly.
- **Unusable dates.** 3 contracts have no usable `dateSigned` and are dropped from every table.
- **Impossible and out-of-window years.** 21,456 contracts are dated outside 2018 to 2026 and are dropped.
  21,390 of them are dated 2000 to 2017, where PPCM coverage is thin (3,993 contracts in 2016, 13,222 in
  2017, against 14,985 in 2018). The other 66 carry impossible years: 0200 to 0226 (13 records), 1901 to
  1992 (50), and 2027, 2029, 2108 (one each). The full year-by-year count is in `summary3.json` under
  `outsideRangeByYear`. Any date whose month is not 01 to 12 is treated as unusable rather than
  out of range.
- **Missing values.** 867 contracts carry no value. They count as contracts and add zero to value sums,
  so they pull median and mean figures down slightly in the months where they sit.
- **No shard failed** on this run; `failedShards` in `summary3.json` is empty.
- Nothing here uses `tenderAppealsInfo`, the free-text appeals narrative that story #1 had to classify by
  hand. This story needs no free-text field.

## Caveats

- **Signed, not spent.** PPCM publishes the date a contract was signed, not the date money moved. A
  framework or annual contract signed in January and drawn down all year loads January. This is the single
  largest reason to read the January peak as a signing calendar rather than a spending calendar, and it
  cuts the same way against reading December as one.
- **Procedure form, not legal ground.** PPCM records which procedure was used, never why. An urgent award
  can be a genuine emergency, a court or government instruction, or a schedule that slipped. Nothing here
  is a finding about legality.
- **The Ministry of Health caveat carries over.** Its January single-source volume is state-order payments
  to public hospitals, which PPCM records as single-source contracts. It is an accounting convention as
  much as a procurement choice.
- **2026 is year to date**, signatures to 6 September 2026. Its monthly shares use a partial denominator
  and are excluded from every pooled figure and every December comparison.
- **The 2020 and 2021 Decembers are unusual years, not a pattern.** December 2020 held 29.03 percent of
  that year's value with the Ministry of Health at 64.03 percent of the month and the City Planning
  Committee at 23.5 percent; 91.28 percent of December 2020's value was signed from 22 December. Treat
  those two years as events, not as evidence of a recurring December rush.
- Buyer counts move when a body is renamed or re-registered under a new tax ID.
- Nothing here was reviewed by a lawyer or by PPCM. Corrections are welcome; rerun `analyze3.mjs` against
  the next weekly index and the figures update.
